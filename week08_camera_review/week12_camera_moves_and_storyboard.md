# 🎥 12.1 Camera Moves

## 📚 What Are Camera Moves?
Key definitions (from the Columbia University Film Glossary):

- **Track / Truck Shot**  
  The camera physically moves through space (left/right/forward/back) on tracks, wheels, or a stabilizer.

- **Pan**  
  Camera **rotates left or right** from a fixed position on a tripod or swivel head.

- **Tilt**  
  Camera **rotates up or down** from a fixed position.

- **Zoom**  
  Camera stays put, but the **lens changes focal length**, magnifying or widening the image.  
  Example: zoom clip inspired by *Taxi Driver*  
  – https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=boKraRoVh5k

These tools affect:

- what the audience sees  
- when they see it  
- how they feel about the moment  

---

## 🧭 When to Add Camera Moves
You decide whether to move the camera. Ask:

- What is the **story reason** for this move?  
- Does it reveal **new information**?  
- Does it follow a character’s **emotion or action**?  
- Does it shift our **attention** from one element to another?  

Think of the camera as a **motivated character**:

- it reacts  
- it discovers  
- it emphasizes  

For more insight, see master cinematographer **M. David Mullen** on *when* and *why* to move the camera (Canvas link).

---

## 📝 How to Storyboard Camera Moves
Read: **Chapter on Storyboarding Camera Moves** (Canvas download).

In your storyboard, you can:

- use **arrows** to indicate pans, tilts, and tracking  
- write labels like *PAN LEFT*, *TILT UP*, *TRACK IN*, *ZOOM OUT*  
- break a move into **multiple panels** if the shot changes significantly  
- add **brief notes** below panels explaining motivation  
  - e.g., “PAN RIGHT to reveal friend entering frame”  
  - e.g., “TRACK IN as character realizes truth”  

Goal: another person should be able to look at your boards and understand exactly:

- how the camera moves  
- why it moves  
- where the move begins and ends

# 🖼 12.3 Rough Storyboard

## 🎬 Storyboard Requirements
**Assignment:** Create a storyboard for your **3-page script**.

You are the director. Use the storyboard to communicate your **visual plan**:

Your storyboard should:

- include **at least 18 shots**  
  - you may repeat shot types to show coverage  
- cover **all scenes** in your 3-page script  
- show what you learned about **shot types** and **coverage**  
- indicate **shot and scene numbers**  
- include **notes** under panels to clarify action and movement  
- show **camera moves** where relevant (pans, tilts, tracks, zooms)  

You can use:

- drawn **thumbnails**  
- **found images** (screenshots, photos, etc.)  
- **staged photos** of friends or yourself  
- **AI-generated images**  

You may create your storyboard in:

- **CeltX** (recommended for easy formatting)  
- another digital tool  
- or by hand, then scan/photograph and combine into a PDF  

---

## 📂 What to Turn In
Upload to Canvas:

- a **PDF** of your **rough storyboard**

If using CeltX:

1. Build your storyboard in CeltX.  
2. Export / download as **PDF**.  
3. Upload that PDF to the **12.3 Rough Storyboard** assignment.

This is your **first draft** storyboard. It does not have to be perfect.  
The goal is to **try** visualizing your film so you can revise later.

---

## 🤝 Peer Feedback & Rubric
This assignment is **Credit / No Credit**.

- You will receive feedback from **classmates** in next week’s workshop.  
- The instructor may also comment or annotate your PDF.  

Peer feedback is guided by three main questions:

1. **What sticks with you?**  
   - Be specific: moments, settings, interactions, actions, dialogue, plot points, shot choices.

2. **Where did you get lost / confused / zone out?**  
   - Again, be specific about the exact moment you lost focus and the moment you *tuned back in*.

3. **What’s the story you heard?**  
   - Summarize the story you understood from the storyboard alone.

Use this feedback to decide:

- what to **change or clarify**  
- where to **adjust camera moves or coverage**  
- how to prepare for the **Final Storyboard** (due later in the semester)
