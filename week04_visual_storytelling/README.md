### Module Objective

---

### Module Overview  

#### Visual Storytelling
- Visual storytelling is one of the oldest forms of human communication (ex: cave paintings ~44,000 years ago).  
- Today, storyboards are a direct continuation of narrative-pictorial communication.  
- **Challenge:** It’s harder to capture a person’s *thoughts* in an image than their *actions*.  

#### Using Descriptive Language
- **DIRECT (External):** visible characteristics (room furnishings, light, clothing, body shape, skin/hair, etc).  
- **INDIRECT (Internal):** expressed through actions, goals, and responses to stress or antagonism.  

You will:  
- Recognize storytelling in pictorial forms.  
- Learn artistic and technical aspects of character descriptions.  
- Consider stereotypes vs. objectives in character development.  
- Create a **two-sentence description** of your protagonist using external + action traits.  

---

## 4.1 Telling Stories with Pictures: Narrative-Pictorial Forms  

**What defines a narrative picture?**  
- Includes **characters** (often human), **recognizable forms**, and **interactions**.  
- Shows interaction over time (sequences).  
- Can be layered with text to clarify meaning.  

**Not every picture is narrative:**  
- Abstract images may suggest mood, palette, or ideas, but don’t *tell a story*.  

---

## 4.3 Visuals > Words: Character Development  

### Character Descriptions
- Simple, 1–2 sentences long.  
- Appear in treatments and help casting directors.  

**External traits:** appearance, age, gender, race, ethnicity.  
**Action traits:** “Character is action” (Aristotle). These reveal personality, objective, beliefs, class/status.  

### Developing Character
- Avoid relying only on external stereotypes.  
- If stereotypes exist in audience minds, use them with purpose.  
- Strong objectives = strong characters. Always ask: *Do you know what your protagonist wants?*

## 4.5 Visuals > Words: Setting Description  

### Why Pictures?
- Research 3 pictures that feel like your setting.  
- Pictures inspire descriptive language for mood, light, and atmosphere.  

### Describing Setting
Include only details that advance story:  
- Indoor/outdoor  
- Weather  
- Bright/dim  
- Quiet/loud  
- Crowded/empty  
- Time period  
- Light (warm/cool, diffuse/specific) – a key tool for expressing **mood**.
