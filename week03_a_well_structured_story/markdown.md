## 3.3 3-Act Structure & Plot Points  

**Three Acts Timeline**  
- **Act I (¼ length)** → Setup  
- **Act II (½ length)** → Main Action  
- **Act III (¼ length)** → Resolution  

**Plot Points (1 per act):**  
1. **Inciting Incident** → conflict begins, protagonist’s goal emerges  
2. **Point of No Return** → irreversible choice, protagonist commits  
3. **Climax** → peak tension, conflict resolution imminent  

| Act | Structural Importance | Length (3-page story) | Plot Point |
|-----|-----------------------|------------------------|------------|
| 1   | Setup: introduce world & characters | ¾ page | Inciting Incident |
| 2   | Main Action: core conflict unfolds | 1.5 pages | Point of No Return |
| 3   | Resolution: outcome revealed | ¾ page | Climax |

**Example: The Big House**  
- **Act I**: Boy in Yemen revolution (inciting incident: finds Big Man’s key)  
- **Act II**: Boy takes over house (point of no return: jumping on bed → picture loosens)  
- **Act III**: Resolution (climax: picture falls, he’s discovered, must run)

## 3.5 Dramatic Tension in Your Story  
- Definition: **pleasurable anticipation** of what happens next  
- Built through:  
  - Protagonist (driver)  
  - Objective (need/goal)  
  - Antagonist (obstacle)  

✍️ Write down for your story:  
- Protagonist  
- Their Objective  
- Antagonist
