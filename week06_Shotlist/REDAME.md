## 🧭 Overview
A **shotlist** is your production blueprint — it ensures full **coverage** of every scene in your script. It defines what the camera must capture, from master shots to cut-ins.  
While animation artists draw each shot, live-action filmmakers rely on the shotlist to communicate their vision to the crew.

Even if you work in animation or hybrid formats, understanding **coverage** helps you plan storytelling visually.

---

## 🎥 Key Concepts

### 1. Coverage
**Definition:** Selecting shots that capture all the action of your script through the camera.  
**Good coverage** means:
- Enough footage from enough angles for editing options  
- Avoiding excessive or unnecessary shots  
- Maintaining the director’s creative vision  

**Standard formula for coverage:**
1. **Wide** — establish location  
2. **Medium(s)** — develop story and dialogue  
3. **Close-ups** — intensify emotional action  

🔗 **Watch:** [Coverage Explained (YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3nsWXX0zkEM&t=540s)

---

### 2. Shot Sizes
| Type | Description | Purpose |
|------|--------------|----------|
| **Close-Up (CU)** | Focuses on face or detail | Emotional intensity |
| **Medium (MS)** | Frames from waist up | Dialogue, balance |
| **Wide (WS)** | Shows full body and setting | Establishes context |

🎬 **Resource:** StudioBinder — *Shot Size Examples*

---

### 3. Shot Types (Framing)
Framing defines **how** the subject is arranged:
- Number of characters (single, two-shot, etc.)  
- Camera angle (high, low, eye-level)  
- Emphasis on setting or subject  

🎬 **Resource:** StudioBinder — *Framing Examples*

## 🧩 Assignment — Create Your Shotlist

**Task:**  
Lay out your shotlist based on your script. Include shot sizes and types that effectively tell your story.

### ✅ Requirements:
- 6 shots (or fewer) per page of script  
- Include:
  - Page #
  - Scene #
  - Shot #
  - Shot size
  - Shot type / framing
  - Description of action or subject  
- Each shot listed **once**, even if reused  
- Ensure **coverage** (master + mediums + close-ups)

**Tip:** Plan your shots using this visual flow:
> Wide → Medium → Close-Up → Insert (if needed)
